# 1. Attributes

- Might (strength) (physical strength)
- Agility (Dexterity) (physical agility)
- Intellect (Inteligence) (mental strength)
- Wits (Wisdom / Charisma) (mental agility)

Attributes tie in to skills giving them re-rolls based on the Attribute level.
- Might -> Athletics, Intimidation, Crafting, etc..
- Agility -> Stealth, Thievery, Acrobatics, etc..
- Intellect -> Persuasion, Concentration, History, etc..
- Wits -> Perception, Survival, Medicine, etc..

# 2. Secondary attributes


### Max health

Health is derived from constitution. There isn't a singular health pool but several - one for each body part: head, torso, 2x arm, gut and 2x leg. Health pool does not end at 0 rather it goes to the negative number of max health. After passing 0 in to the negatives the player will receive a penalty depending on which body part went under 0.

Health calculation: 10 + 2 * Might
The total HP pool will be destributes to all of the body parts.

- Head -> total HP / 5
- Arms -> total HP / 5
- Chest -> total HP / 3
- Abdomen -> total HP / 4
- Legs -> total HP / 4

That would mean that at level 1 of might the total HP pool is 12 which would translate to:

- Head -> 2 HP
- Arms -> 2 HP
- Chest -> 4 HP
- Abdomen -> 3 HP
- Legs -> 3 HP

### Movement speed

Move distance per turn. Base is 5 squares per turn plus a bonus based on Agility. When wearing armor speed gets reduced.

| LVL   | 1 - 2 | 3 - 4 | 5 - 6 | 7 - 8 |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Speed | 0     | +1    | +2    | +3    |

### Initiative bonus

To base initiative rolls at the start of combat (d20) the player will add a modifier based on Agility level + Wits level

### Action points

Base action points start at 2 AP. AP can be used for attacks, movement and reactions during combat. To get more AP the players will have to upgrade their Agility and/or Wits.

- base AP + 1 -> player needs 4+ in Agility OR Wits
- base AP + 2 -> player need 6+ in Agility AND Wits

### Carry capacity

Determines how many kilograms the character is able to carry on them. The calculation: 5 + Might * 2

- Minor Encumbrance (> Carry capacity) - reduced speed, -1 stamina
- Major Encumbrance (> Carry capacity * 1,5) - halved, -2 stamina

Nobody ever cares about carry capacity so not sure if this is relevant.

### Stamina (Energy)

Stamina pool is determined by adding Might and Intellect and then devide it by 2 (Might + Intellect) / 2. When reaching Stamina 0 the character falls unconscious. When stamina is low the player gets penalty to successes rolled.

| Stamina lost    | 1 - 2 | 3 - 4 | 5 - 6 | 7 - 8 |
| --------------- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Success penalty | -1    | -2    | -3    | -4    |

# 4. Character creation

Every character starts with all attributes at level 1, 5 skills of the players choosing ranging from level 1 to level 5. Players will have to also fill out their max health, speed, carrying capacity and luck.

Players will also choose their characters name, race, gender, what their character looks like and their backstory.

# 5. Character progression

After creating a character and going on a adventure the players will receive experience for successfully preforming tasks, defeating enemies or solving puzzles. XP is can then be spent on upgrading the players Attributes or skills. Players have to spend double of what the particular attribute or skill currently has to raise it's level by 1. The story teller gives out the XP to the players.

Probably will change in the future.

Another way players can progress is by obtaining new better gear or new spells

# 6. Rolling the dice

### Success

Whenever the player rolls some combination of dice they look at how many successes they have rolled. The more successes the better the roll.

After rolling the player counts how many dice have the value of 6 or more. They count the number of dice with that value and that will be the number of successes rolled.

Example: player rolls 3, 5, 6, 2, 9 -> that will add up to 2 successes.

# 7. Combat flow

### Initiative

Initiative determines the order in which players and monsters take their turns. At the start of every combat all players and monsters roll 1D20 and add their speed to the roll to determine their initiative. 

### Round

Combat round is a complete cycle in combat during which each player and monster takes their turn in order of initiative, using their available Action Points (AP) to perform actions such as moving, attacking, or using abilities. After reaching the bottom of the initiative order, the round returns to the top, allowing participants with remaining AP to continue taking actions. This process repeats until no one has any AP left. Only then is all AP replenished for the next round.
___

# 8. Combat actions and reactions

# Actions

Players can preform actions during their turn.

### Attacking

When players want to preform an attack action they have to spend 1 AP. After spending 1 AP the player rolls dice depending on the level of the relevant the attribute depending on the weapon. The players can also add their skill dice if they are skilled in that particular weapon.

Based on the number of successes rolled the player rolls that many damage dice depending on the weapon.

Attacking multiple times with one weapon during a single turn will result in a -1 success penalty for each consecutive roll.

### Moving

Players can spend 1 AP to move 5 meters / squares + movement from their speed in their turn. Each consecutive movement action in a single turn will result in a 1 meter penalty.

### Pushing

Player can spend 1 AP to push their opponent. Both the player and the enemy roll their strength dice. If the player rolls more successes they'll push the enemy that many meters / squares depending on how many more successes they rolled.

### Blocking

When holding a weapon or a shield the player can choose a location which to passively protect. If the attacker hits the protected area the attack has -1 success when defender is blocking with a weapon. Shields can protect multiple places at ones and have their ows stats for negating successes. While blocking the player cannot use the weapon or the shield to parry or to attack. 

# Reactions

Players can preform reaction during other players or monsters turns under the specific reaction conditions.

### Parrying

If a player is being attacked by a physical weapon (including ranged weapons) they can spend 1 AP to parry the attackers attack by rolling an opposed roll with their weapon skill dice subctracting the rolled successes.

# 11. Positioning during combat

### Flanking

While flanking the the enemy the attacker gains +1 success to their rolls. In order to flank the enemy there has to be one hero standing behind and one standing on front of the enemy.

### Up against the wall

If a character has a wall behind him and someone is attacking him the attacker has +1 to their success. Essentially the same as flanking but the wall acts as a second player.

### Full and half cover

Full cover gives the ranged attacker -2 successes to their attack roll. For example standing behind a corner of a building. While behind a half cover ranged attacker has -1 success to their attack roll. For example hiding behind a box.
